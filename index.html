ABOUT THE COURSE

AI gives a response.
You decide what to do with it.

In the sample, you see the request and three images AI generated from it for a game night invitation. You decide which response fits what you would use.

In AI & YOU, students practice this with real AI tools. They make images, check information, plan activities, and create things they care about.

They also choose what information to share, consider other people’s permission, and set limits before a tool takes action.

What students do
in the 8 sessions

Students use approved AI tools in the course. Prepared examples are available when live access is unavailable. Security, ethics, and supervised AI actions are part of the eight sessions—not extra lessons added on top. Combine sessions or extend a project. Open a title for a short example.

01What Is AI in My World?Start with a game-night image, then make an invitation with AI.+

What students do: Look at the prepared game-night image, choose an activity you enjoy, and use AI to make an invitation. Note what you asked for and what you did with the response.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

A first creation and a note about your choices.

02Check InformationCheck a claim before passing it on.+

What students do: Use AI to draft a message about a familiar activity. Check one claim against a dated notice or another useful source, then decide what to use, change, or leave unclear.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

A comparison showing what was checked, used, or left open.

03Compare VersionsUse the same prompt to compare two responses.+

What students do: Use the same prompt for two responses. Compare details, read a short note from a tool maker, and ask who or what is missing without guessing why.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

Images or descriptions with notes about details and unanswered questions.

04Plan SomethingChoose useful help and limit what you share.+

What students do: Plan an event or activity. Select useful information and compare access options. Decide what may be drafted and what needs permission before action.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

A plan and a short record of information and permission choices.

05Try Another WayChange a request and see what happens.+

What students do: Compare a harmless source notice with and without an extra instruction, then try a small revision of your own.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

Two versions and a note about what changed—or did not.

06Think About SharingConsider what to use, what needs permission, and what to explain.+

What students do: Make an original lyric, fictional character, or message. Consider who is affected, what needs permission, and what a recipient should know.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

A creation and the conditions for using or sharing it.

07Set Limits on ActionsSeparate a draft from an action. Pause and ask for help.+

What students do: Use live AI to make a draft, then review prepared action records. Choose limits, separate what a tool proposed from what the records show, and practice getting help.

EXAMPLE OF THEIR WORK

A next-step note about what happened, what is unknown, and who can help.

Try the sending decision
08Bring It TogetherChoose earlier work, make one small piece, and explain your choices.+

What students do: Choose selected earlier work, then use AI to make a small scene, storyboard, or audio piece. Show AI’s part, explain a choice, and compare your closing reflections with your first notes. Sharing is optional.

WAYS TO TAKE PART

Turning on cameras is not required.

A camera being on, or off, does not by itself tell you whether a student is engaged or interested. Students may be listening, thinking, writing, or taking part in another way. AI & YOU offers different ways to participate.

A

Talk

Ask a question, share an example, or explain a choice.

B

Write

Use chat, write a private note, or add a comment to a piece of work.

C

Make

Change a line, draw an idea, or add something to the group’s project.

What does a school or nonprofit need?

A facilitator, the course materials, and approved AI access. Students can share devices or direct an instructor-operated tool.

The core agent activity is a labeled simulation, with a separate live AI drafting step. It does not require students to connect email, buy an agent plan, or open new personal accounts. Any optional live-agent demonstration needs a controlled, approved setup.

Does the course have to run over eight days?

No. The full plan has eight one-hour sessions, but they do not have to happen on eight separate days. You can combine sessions or spend longer on a project. A shorter program will cover fewer activities.

Is this the full course? Is the AI here live?

This website is a public sample. Its lyric and sending activity are prepared examples, not live AI. No messages are sent.

Students use approved AI tools throughout the full course. The action simulation is identified separately from that live work.

How do you look at what students are learning?

Students retain a few requests, responses, edits, plans, and permission or action decisions. They compare early and later work and explain what changed or stayed similar.

Instructors consider what supported participation and what got in the way. Catching an error is one observation, not proof that learning occurred. Keeping a useful response can also be meaningful.

Edition 1.1 is a review and pilot edition. The complete revised course has not yet been field-tested as a whole.

What about personal advice, AI companions, or environmental costs?

Optional fictional cases help students consider privacy and support in AI conversations, and the resources or people involved in making AI content. These replace an extension or use extra time. Students do not have to share private conversations or personal experiences.

Accessibility and privacy

You can use the page with a keyboard. There is no timer, automatic audio, or video. You can explore the course without finishing the activity. Text versions are provided for the sample document images.

The site is built with WCAG 2.2 Level AA as its accessibility goal. That is a goal, not a certification. Further screen-reader and user testing is still needed. Read W3C’s guidance on checking accessibility.

This preview has no account, advertising, or analytics. It does not send your choices or writing to Ypifany or an AI service. It does not save your writing for another visit. Your browser may remember form text through its own features. The website host may keep normal access logs. Other websites have their own privacy rules. An embedded YouTube video connects to YouTube or Google when it loads or plays. Those services may receive standard connection data under their privacy policy.
